Featured Case Study

HemoScan

AI-Assisted Non-Invasive Hemoglobin Estimation Prototype

Live Prototype Code Repository
Stack: Python Computer Vision Photoplethysmography (PPG) React Replit
Research Prototype — Not a Medical Device

Research prototype, not a medical device. This software is developed solely for academic exploration and optical signal experimentation. It is not approved by regulatory bodies and must never be used as a substitute for professional clinical medical advice, certified laboratory blood testing, or clinical diagnosis.

1. The Problem

Traditional hemoglobin testing requires an invasive blood draw via needles, trained phlebotomists, sterile consumables, and physical visits to clinical pathology laboratories. For patients requiring frequent hemoglobin monitoring, as well as populations in rural or low-resource settings, this creates substantial barriers in cost, accessibility, and physical discomfort.

2. Engineering Approach

Explored whether smartphone camera sensors can capture microvascular color variations in fingertip capillary beds using photoplethysmography (PPG). When illuminated by the camera LED flash, blood volume oscillations alter light absorption across red, green, and blue optical channels. The prototype extracts RGB intensity ratios, normalizes baseline signal noise, and applies regression modeling to estimate hemoglobin concentration ranges.

3. Current Prototype Results

Built a functional web application prototype deployed on Replit that processes live camera feeds or frame captures, isolates the region of interest, computes colorimetric intensity shifts, and presents estimated hemoglobin figures alongside standard reference categories.

4. Known Limitations & Edge Cases

The system remains a research prototype with significant constraints: high sensitivity to ambient light inconsistency, hardware variability across different smartphone camera sensors (auto-exposure and white-balance adjustments), skin tone variations, inconsistent finger contact pressure, and a lack of clinical trial validation against gold-standard laboratory hematology analyzers.

5. Key Learnings & Engineering Takeaways

Learned practical computer vision preprocessing, digital signal filtering, techniques for isolating optical noise from biological signals, and the critical ethical responsibility of communicating system limitations through explicit disclaimers in biomedical applications.
